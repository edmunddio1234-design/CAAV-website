What it is

A training lab built by students, built to move.

The Southern University Mobile CAVE (Computer Automated Virtual Environment) is described by the university as a one-of-a-kind, transportable immersive research and training lab, conceived and led by students from concept to completion and outfitted by industry experts.

Immersive learning

Classroom learning expanded through real-world, hands-on virtual experiences.

Research and innovation

A mobile platform for interdisciplinary research and simulation.

Workforce development

Prepares students for high-demand careers in emerging technologies.

Community impact

Brings advanced training and simulation to schools, communities and industry partners.

Fully transportable

The environment rides in the vehicle and goes to the learner.

Bridges three worlds

Academia, industry and government, with wider access to immersive technology.

The thing it actually does

Interactive learning: the learner acts, the room answers.

A video explains. A simulation lets you try, get it wrong, see what happens, and try again. That loop is the product.

DoStep into the scenario. Open the valve, trace the circuit, examine the patient, build the cell.
See the resultThe environment responds right away. The leak spreads. The reading changes. The monitor alarms.
DebriefAn instructor replays the moment and asks why. The mistake becomes the lesson.
RepeatRun it again, harder or different. No materials used up, nobody hurt, no line shut down.

Science lesson

Walk inside the idea

Shrink to the size of a cell, stand in a river delta, watch a reaction from the inside. The student moves things and sees what changes, instead of copying a diagram.

Technical training

Practice the dangerous day safely

A new hire runs the emergency shutdown, spots the hazard, or walks a drone inspection, and a mistake costs nothing. The crew does it again until it is automatic.

Healthcare

Rehearse the rare, high-stakes case

Run the scenario that seldom shows up in a placement: the crash, the surge, the difficult conversation. Teams repeat it and compare notes in the debrief.

What gets in the way, and what removes it

Barriers in science, technical training and healthcare.

This is our read of the barriers, from people who build training. Tell us what we missed in the form below.

Field
The barrier today
What interactive 3D changes
ScienceK-12 and community learning

Barriers

  • Many schools have no lab, or too little equipment, for the experiments the lesson needs.
  • The subject is often too small, too large, too fast, too slow or too dangerous to see.
  • Lessons lean on text and diagrams, so a student who does not connect with dense reading is left behind.
  • A live demo happens once. Missed it, and it is gone.

What changes

  • The truck brings the lab to the school. No building to equip.
  • Make the invisible walkable and the dangerous safe.
  • Learners reach the same idea by seeing, moving and doing, not only by reading.
  • Replay it, slow it down, change one thing and run it again.
Technical trainingPlants, manufacturing, contractors

Barriers

  • Training pulls workers off the line, so every class costs production time.
  • Real equipment is hazardous for beginners and not always free to practice on.
  • Emergencies are rare, so crews seldom rehearse the one that matters most.
  • Sending people to a training center costs travel and time.
  • A shortage of skilled workers: a 2023 industry article put the need at up to 5,400 more workers across New Orleans, Baton Rouge and Lake Charles.

What changes

  • The unit parks at the plant. Short sessions fit into a shift change.
  • Learners practice on a simulated version first, then touch the real one.
  • Run the emergency drill as often as needed, with nothing at risk.
  • One vehicle serves many sites, so the cost spreads across every learner.
  • A veteran or trainee can show real skill in the simulation before the first day on site.
HealthcareClinical skills and emergency response

Barriers

  • Practicing on real patients carries risk, so learners need a safe place to start.
  • Rare, high-stakes cases are hard to schedule in a placement.
  • Simulation labs and manikins are costly and sit in a few fixed locations.
  • Staff on shifts cannot easily leave the floor for a day of training.
  • Rural and small facilities are farthest from the labs.

What changes

  • Learners make and see the mistake in simulation, not at the bedside.
  • Scenarios can be dialed to the case a team most needs to rehearse.
  • The simulation arrives at the hospital or school, so no travel to a fixed lab.
  • Short sessions can be booked around shifts.
  • The unit can be scheduled to reach smaller sites on a tour.

Who can use it

Any place where people must learn something that is hard to practice for real.

Industrial plants and manufacturers

Evidence

What published research says.

PwC study of VR training

Faster, more confident, cheaper at scale

  • Learners trained up to 4 times faster than in a classroom.
  • Up to 275% more confident applying what they learned.
  • Up to 4 times more focused than e-learning peers.
  • Content costs up to 48% more to build, breaks even with classroom at 375 learners, and at 3,000 learners is 52% cheaper.
The study covered a leadership course for new managers in 12 US locations. PwC calls the figures estimates. It is not a plant or hospital study.
Louisiana industrial workforce

The need is large and local

  • $154 billion in industrial projects in the pipeline across New Orleans, Baton Rouge and Lake Charles (economist figure quoted in the article).
  • Up to 5,400 more industry workers needed: 2,000 electricians, 500 instrument technicians, 700 millwrights, 2,200 pipefitters and contract welders.
The article is from December 13, 2023. We will refresh these figures before each sponsor meeting.
