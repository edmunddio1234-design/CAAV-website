Additive Manufacturing Lab (proposed)

Additive manufacturing, proposed.

A planning framework for a VR and hands-on 3D printing training pathway. Not yet approved or funded.

Proposed, not established. This is a planning framework. Nothing here is an approved program, a signed partnership, committed funding or a validated result.

Tagline (proposed)

Train Virtually. Build Confidently. Manufacture the Future.

The Additive Manufacturing and 3D Printing Training Initiative would prepare people for careers in advanced manufacturing, engineering, industrial technology and digital fabrication. It builds on a VR training prototype that lets a beginner explore the parts of a 3D printer and simulate a full print job before touching costly equipment. It would run as a technical pathway inside the wider workforce effort, not as a separate program. It connects to sub-project SP-05 (ADV-MFG).

  • Increase access to advanced manufacturing education.
  • Reduce unnecessary equipment exposure and material waste in introductory training.
  • Build measurable technical competencies through simulations and assessments.
  • Open pathways to further training, industry credentials where available, apprenticeships and jobs.

The VR environment prepares learners. It does not replace physical equipment training or required safety certification.

Two modules

A printer tutorial and a full print job.

Module 1: 3D Printer Components

Learners interact with a virtual printer, identify components, see highlighted parts, get guidance and complete a knowledge check.

Module 2: Complete Print Job

Learners practice the sequence of a print job from preparation to simulated completion, with guidance and feedback.

The prototype used Blender for 3D modeling, Unity 2022 for the application, XR interaction tools and OpenXR, with deployment for Meta Quest and a CAVE-style environment. Reporting of learning records is a recommended extension, not something confirmed in the demonstration. Before adopting it, review software ownership, source-code access, hardware fit, upkeep, cybersecurity and licensing.

One pathway

A specialty inside Advanced Manufacturing.

The AM Lab shares recruitment, case management, employer engagement and reporting with the wider workforce program (Construction and Trades, Healthcare, Advanced Manufacturing, Logistics and Technology). Learners could arrive through schools, community groups, workforce centers, reentry programs or direct application.

The AM Lab would be a section of the main workforce site, with sections for overview, VR demonstration, curriculum, training lab, enrollment, employer partners, sponsor the lab, student projects and a verified-results dashboard. Proposed campaign: Print Your Future. Lines: Learn Before You Build; From Virtual Practice to Real Skills; Sponsor the Next Generation of Makers.

VR simulation completion is recorded separately from hands-on competency. Funders and employers need to see the difference. Individual records would be limited to authorized staff; only totals are shown publicly. The dashboard in the framework (6 of 8 modules, 85% knowledge score, 12 hours VR practice) is demo data only.

Before launch

Safe in the headset and at the printer.

VR: headset hygiene, supervised use, accessibility alternatives, session length, movement boundaries, a plan for motion sickness. Physical printing: hot surfaces, moving parts, electrical safety, ventilation and emissions, materials handling, fire prevention, maintenance. Controls depend on the printers and materials chosen. A support agreement should cover updates, device compatibility, fixes, assessment accuracy and secure data transfer.
